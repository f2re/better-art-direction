# Interface modes

Use this reference to classify the surface before choosing composition, density, imagery, and motion. The same visual treatment is not appropriate for every product.

## Contents

- Operational and administrative software
- Analytical and scientific tools
- Consumer applications
- Commerce and transactional flows
- Editorial and knowledge products
- Marketing and launch pages
- Mobile-first products
- Component-scale work
- Mixed products

## Operational and administrative software

Examples: CRM, personnel systems, document workflows, scheduling, inventory, configuration panels, moderation tools.

Priorities:

- Fast scanning and predictable placement.
- Dense but legible controls.
- Stable navigation and clear work states.
- Visible data ownership, filters, validation, and recovery.
- Minimal decorative interruption.

Prefer:

- Clear regions, aligned tables and forms, restrained surfaces.
- Semantic status color and explicit labels.
- Progressive disclosure for advanced controls.
- Compact typography and stable action zones.

Avoid:

- Marketing-style hero sections inside the application.
- Huge headlines consuming the first viewport.
- Decorative cards around every field or row.
- Motion that delays routine actions.
- Hidden navigation disguised as an aesthetic gesture.

A signature should emerge from the workflow: a spatial document map, a powerful command surface, an unusually clear timeline, or a domain-specific visualization.

## Analytical and scientific tools

Examples: weather dashboards, laboratory software, telemetry, GIS, engineering calculations, monitoring, financial analysis.

Priorities:

- Data integrity, comparability, and temporal or spatial context.
- Dense information without accidental hierarchy.
- Stable legends, units, provenance, and uncertainty.
- Keyboard and expert workflows.

Prefer:

- Data-first composition.
- Monospaced or tabular numerals where useful.
- Purpose-built legends and scales.
- Coordinated views rather than unrelated cards.
- Color used according to measurement meaning, not decoration.

Avoid:

- Generic KPI cards when relationships matter more than isolated totals.
- Decorative gradients that conflict with scientific scales.
- Rounded visual containers that fragment continuous data.
- Invented sample values that could be mistaken for real observations.
- Hiding units, valid time, source, or confidence.

A signature should come from the information itself: a profile, map, timeline, uncertainty band, synchronized cursor, or spatial hierarchy.

## Consumer applications

Examples: personal productivity, communication, media, wellness, travel, learning, social products.

Priorities:

- Immediate orientation and clear next action.
- Emotional fit without obscuring utility.
- Comfortable touch targets and forgiving flows.
- Consistent states and feedback.

Prefer:

- A small number of expressive product moments.
- Familiar interaction patterns with a distinct content or material language.
- Realistic personal content and empty states.
- Motion that confirms state or preserves continuity.

Avoid:

- Making every screen visually novel.
- Excessive onboarding decoration after the first-run phase.
- Generic lifestyle photography unrelated to the actual activity.
- Gamification without a product reason.

A signature can be a content transition, a recognizable object model, a tactile control, or a distinctive way of showing progress.

## Commerce and transactional flows

Examples: shops, booking, subscriptions, checkout, quoting, procurement.

Priorities:

- Trust, product clarity, price transparency, and completion.
- Strong distinction between information and action.
- Clear recovery from validation, stock, payment, and delivery problems.

Prefer:

- Product imagery or evidence that supports the decision.
- Stable totals and visible consequences.
- Specific action labels.
- Restrained promotional accents.

Avoid:

- Hiding total cost or recurring terms.
- Decorative urgency, fabricated scarcity, or unsupported claims.
- Ambiguous buttons such as `Continue` when the consequence is payment.
- Animations that move prices, forms, or confirmation controls.

A signature can come from the product material, comparison method, configuration experience, or delivery visualization.

## Editorial and knowledge products

Examples: documentation, research archives, educational products, news, long-form publishing, internal knowledge bases.

Priorities:

- Reading hierarchy, provenance, navigation, and retrieval.
- Comfortable measure and clear relationships between primary and supporting material.
- Durable URLs, headings, citations, and search.

Prefer:

- Typography-led composition grounded in the content genre.
- Meaningful marginalia, annotations, timelines, or diagrams.
- Quiet surfaces and explicit navigation.

Avoid:

- Newspaper styling when the content is not editorial.
- Decorative numbering with no structural meaning.
- Excessive cards that break continuous reading.
- Truncation that hides important source information.

A signature can be a citation system, document map, reading progress model, or domain-specific annotation language.

## Marketing and launch pages

Examples: product introduction, campaign, event, portfolio, public brand page.

Priorities:

- A clear proposition, credible evidence, and a coherent narrative.
- Product-specific imagery and language.
- One memorable opening argument.

Prefer:

- A hero that acts as a thesis rather than a template.
- Real product artifacts, demonstrations, or subject-specific materials.
- Section rhythm based on the sales argument, not a fixed `hero → three cards → testimonials → CTA` sequence.
- One controlled visual risk.

Avoid:

- Fabricated customer counts, logos, reviews, or performance claims.
- Generic purple-blue gradients, floating blobs, icon grids, and empty bento layouts.
- Repeated `Learn more` links.
- Meta labels such as `SECTION 01` used only as decoration.

A signature can be a live demonstration, product artifact, strong typographic construction, or one subject-specific visual system.

## Mobile-first products

Priorities:

- Reachability, continuity, safe areas, keyboard behavior, and real device constraints.
- Clear primary action without covering content.
- Resilience to localization and text scaling.

Prefer:

- Bottom or thumb-reachable controls when appropriate to the platform.
- Content-led collapse rather than desktop sections squeezed vertically.
- Native interaction expectations.

Avoid:

- Desktop card grids simply stacked into a long feed.
- Fixed viewport heights that fail with browser chrome or keyboards.
- Tiny icon-only controls.
- edge-to-edge actions that ignore safe areas.

A signature should survive one-handed use and should not depend on hover.

## Component-scale work

Examples: one button, select, dialog, table row, date picker, card, chart control.

Do not invent a page-level art direction. Inherit the surrounding system and define only:

- Component role and hierarchy.
- Surface, shape, icon, and type relationship.
- Default, hover, focus, active, disabled, loading, error, and success states where applicable.
- Responsive and localization behavior.
- How the component contributes to the existing product signature.

Skip hero, navigation, section rhythm, and page-level imagery decisions.

## Mixed products

Many products combine a public marketing site, authenticated application, documentation, and administration. Classify each surface separately, then preserve a shared brand family through type, color roles, icon language, and material cues.

Do not force identical density or composition across modes. A marketing page can be expressive while the authenticated application remains efficient. The relationship should be recognizable without making every surface behave the same way.
